What is Combinatorics?

Combinatorics is a branch of mathematics concerning the study of finite or countable discrete structures. It involves counting, arranging, and selecting objects according to specific rules.

The two fundamental concepts in combinatorics are permutations (arrangements where order matters) and combinations (selections where order doesn't matter).

🔄

Permutations

What are Permutations?

A permutation is an arrangement of objects in a specific order. The order matters in permutations. For example, ABC and BAC are different permutations of the same three letters.

Formula: P(n, r) = n! / (n - r)!

Where n is the total number of items and r is the number of items to arrange.

Example:

How many ways can you arrange 3 books from a shelf of 5 books?
Answer: P(5, 3) = 5! / (5-3)! = 5! / 2! = 60 ways

Combinations

What are Combinations?

A combination is a selection of objects where the order doesn't matter. For example, selecting {A, B, C} is the same as selecting {C, B, A}.

Formula: C(n, r) = n! / (r! × (n - r)!)

Where n is the total number of items and r is the number of items to select.

Result:

Example:

How many ways can you choose 3 books from a shelf of 5 books?
Answer: C(5, 3) = 5! / (3! × 2!) = 10 ways

Formula Reference

Factorial (n!)

n! = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 2 × 1

The product of all positive integers up to n. By definition, 0! = 1

Permutation P(n, r)

P(n, r) = n! / (n - r)!

Number of ways to arrange r items from n items (order matters)

Combination C(n, r)

C(n, r) = n! / (r! × (n - r)!)

Number of ways to choose r items from n items (order doesn't matter)

🔑 Key Difference:

Permutation: Order matters (ABC ≠ BAC)

Combination: Order doesn't matter ({A,B,C} = {C,B,A})
